Source text, shaped

Namaste' through the lens of spirit

1. Name

In Sanskrit :

  • Nāma (naama) literally means the English word Name,
  • the sibilant 's' is the filler sound,
  • Te' means to you.
2. Outer form

Thus anyone who says Nāma-s-te' worships the named outer form - no wonder the fixation on beautiful bodies in modern Yoga studios.

3. Inner spirit

By pronouncing it correctly as

  • Nama it indicates the inner spirit, not the outer form,

so Namaste' becomes a reminder of divine inner consciousness.

Expanded source content

Spiritualized approach

Spiritualization means actively search for, or even inducing, a spirit, into all of life so that it has a higher evolutionary purpose, beyond just existing.

This makes it is easier to :

  • go beyond the individual form that is presented,
  • and look at the spirit within the other,
  • and how it interacts with ours.

This is essentially why the Yoga Namaste'.

Multiplicity to single spirit

Further, in the initial stages, it's not just one spirit that each possesses, in fact it is a multiplicity of them, and there is a particular process described in Patanjali, and then eventually all spirits collapse into the one single spirit - when the whole of it becomes duh obvious.

Not Spirituality

Spiritual-ity, where the -ity makes it an ideology like in Christian-ity, or for that matter any -ism, wherein the spirit behind the original idea is fossilized to conform to a particular thought at a particular point in time.

A fossilized spirit is of course an oxymoron, because spirit can never be dead, as we consider it the source of life itself - a kind of life that has a certain kind of evolutionary purpose.

So in this site we are changing all such nomenclature right now.
