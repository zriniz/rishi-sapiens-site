Being Conscious

Consciousness is approached anthropocentrically as the state in which phenomena are experienced.

To be conscious is not merely to process information, but to inhabit experience. The concern here is not only with cognition, but with the fact of felt existence — the immediacy through which the world appears, is interpreted, and becomes meaningful.

The argument begins from lived experience. Human beings do not simply register the world as mechanisms. We undergo it, suffer it, interpret it, enjoy it, and evolve through it.

Meaning of Consciousness

The word conscious is tied to being well informed and able to respond wisely.

The term is connected here to the Latin root consci, illustrated through the figure of the consigliere — one who is both well informed and capable of sound counsel. Two markers emerge from this framing:

  • Awareness — being informed, receptive, and perceptive
  • Responsiveness — the ability to act wisely in relation to what is known

Consciousness thus is not treated as vague mysticism, but as a lived conjunction of awareness and response.

The Mind

The mind is the experiencing mechanism of phenomena and the container of consciousness.

Mind is treated as the locus in which experience is gathered, organized, interpreted, and projected. It is not merely a storage unit of thought. It is the operative field in which consciousness takes shape as lived reality.

In this sense, the mind is both container and instrument — receiving phenomena, rendering them plausible, and constructing the internal world through which external reality is known.

Intelligence

Intelligence is tied to curiosity, self-organization, and the ability to read between the lines.

Curiosity

Curiosity is one of the earliest markers of intelligence. It drives pattern recognition, learning, adaptation, and exploration beyond passive survival.

Markers

Intelligence is marked here not only by formal reasoning, but by the capacity to self-organize, discern patterns, and infer what is not immediately explicit.

Human Intelligence

Human intelligence may be measured through many partial quotients — IQ, physical quotient, EQ, aesthetic quotient, and broader notions of multiple intelligence — but none of these alone exhaust the mind’s actual depth.

Natural Intelligence

Natural intelligence is framed through awareness and responsiveness. Intelligence exists wherever systems perceive, adapt, coordinate, and evolve in relation to circumstance.

Awareness

Awareness detects pattern and context.

Responsiveness

Responsiveness acts within pattern and context.

The Sanskrit term Buddhi is central here: discernment, the power to read between the lines.

Sentience

Sentience is approached as an intermediate threshold toward fuller consciousness.

Sentient Things

Sentience need not be granted indiscriminately to all matter from the start. It may instead be treated as an emergent threshold: a deepening capacity of systems to register and respond.

Sentient Creatures

Science is referenced as locating the emergence of consciousness at a critical mass of appropriately connected neurons. In this line of thinking, the threshold begins somewhere low in the biological chain, perhaps around simple creatures such as slugs or snails, and then intensifies through more complex nervous systems.

The Hard Problem

The inquiry turns to the relation between consciousness and materiality, and to two distinct approaches.

Consciousness

The hard problem remains: how does subjective experience arise, and what is the relation between felt life and material process?

Materiality

Materiality cannot simply be dismissed. Consciousness is encountered through embodied forms and organized substrates.

The Algorithmic Way

The algorithmic way analyzes consciousness from the outside, decomposes it into formal structures, and may someday attempt to deliver it as a consumable product.

Generative Way

The generative way attempts something else: to induce consciousness internally, cultivate it directly, and only then externalize its forms.

The proposed generative sequence moves through:

  1. biological wetware
  2. chemical soupware
  3. physicalized mathematics

Complex Adaptive Systems

Evolution is treated as a property of complex adaptive systems, not merely of biological species.

Meanings

Complex adaptive systems are systems that learn, self-organize, respond to environment, and generate novelty over time.

Evolution

Evolution therefore extends beyond Darwinian biology. It applies wherever patterns persist, vary, adapt, and complexify.

Emergence of Novelty

Novelty emerges from interaction, tension, adaptation, and the combinatorial richness of evolving systems.

Thermodynamics and Evolution

Thermodynamics does not negate evolution; rather, local order and increasing complexity arise through the flow and expenditure of energy.

Probability and Evolution

Probability shapes what pathways become plausible or improbable, while adaptive systems continually alter those pathways through feedback.

Avoiding Black Swans

Greater awareness and responsiveness increase the ability to anticipate and mitigate catastrophic discontinuities.

Coevolving The Universe

Evolution becomes coevolution when systems transform not in isolation, but through reciprocal relation with one another and with the larger field.

Universal Intelligence

The culmination of this line of thought is universal intelligence — an intelligence diffused through evolving systems, forms, and relations, rather than confined to an isolated human mind.
